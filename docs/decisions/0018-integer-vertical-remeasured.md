# 0018. Integer-vertical, re-measured: it is not "never worse"

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-25
- **Supersedes:** [0015](0015-integer-vertical-mode.md)
- **Superseded by:** -

[ADR-0014](0014-display-modes-and-default.md) stands unchanged. This replaces
[ADR-0015](0015-integer-vertical-mode.md), whose decisions survive but whose
evidence did not.

## Context

ADR-0015 was measured on 2026-08-24. The core selection landed on 2026-08-25 and
replaced three of the cores it had measured. Nothing detected that, because its
table was keyed on the *system*, and a system does not report an aspect ratio -
a specific core build does.

The table is now generated by measurement from binaries verified against
`CORES.md`: [core-facts.md](../reference/core-facts.md). Three of six rows had
gone false.

| System | ADR-0015 | Measured now | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis | 1.3333 | **1.5238** | PicoDrive rejected for Genesis Plus GX |
| SNES | 1.3333 | **1.5842** PAL, **1.3061** NTSC | snes9x 1.63 dropped for snes9x2010 |
| NES | 1.2190 | **1.3061** | FCEUmm rebuilt from libretro's buildbot |
| Game Boy | 1.1111 | 1.1111 | unchanged |
| GBA | 1.5000 | 1.5000 | unchanged |
| PC Engine | 1.2000 | 1.2000 | unchanged |

ADR-0015 also had one row per system. That cannot be right: **SNES reports
1.5842 for PAL content and 1.3061 for NTSC**, from the same binary. The aspect
is a property of the content, not of the system or the core.

### What the modes actually produce

Measured on the panel, 2026-08-25, rects as Diatom computes them:

| Content | integer | integer-vertical | Shape error, integer | Shape error, i-v | Panel used, integer → i-v |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NES | 768x672 | **878x672** | -12.5% | +0.03% | 65.6% → **75.0%** |
| SNES NTSC | 768x672 | **878x672** | -12.5% | +0.03% | 65.6% → **75.0%** |
| PC Engine | 768x729 | **875x729** | -12.2% | +0.03% | 71.2% → **81.1%** |
| Game Boy | 800x720 | 800x720 | exact | exact | 73.2% → 73.2% |
| GBA | 960x640 | 960x640 | exact | exact | 78.1% → 78.1% |
| **SNES PAL** | 768x672 | **710x448** | -27.9% | +0.03% | 65.6% → **40.4%** |
| **Genesis** | **1024x768** | **878x576** | -12.5% | +0.03% | 100% → **64.3%** |

## The claim that failed

ADR-0015 said, of integer-vertical:

> For square-pixel content it collapses to exactly what plain integer gives, so
> **it is never the worse of the two**.

**That is false**, and the last two rows are why. When the reported aspect is
wide enough, the shape-correct width at vertical factor *N* overflows the panel,
so the mode drops to *N-1* and loses a whole factor. SNES PAL falls from three
vertical multiples to two; Genesis from four to three, giving up a rect that
filled the panel exactly.

Both are cases the old cores never produced. PicoDrive reported every Sega
system as 320x240 at 1.3333 - a fixed canvas, which is why it was rejected - and
snes9x 1.63 reported 1.3333 regardless of region. The wide aspects that break
the claim arrived *with the more truthful cores*. Better measurement made the
mode look worse, which is the correct direction for that to happen and is worth
stating plainly.

## Decision

**Both of ADR-0015's decisions stand. Its guarantee does not.**

1. **The default stays `stretch`.** The corrected numbers strengthen this rather
   than weaken it. Integer is exact for two of seven rows and wrong by 12-28%
   for the other five; stretch is now only **+2.1%** off on NES, where ADR-0015
   measured +9.4%.

2. **`integer-vertical` stays**, and earns its place: shape-exact to within
   0.03% everywhere, while using more of the panel than plain integer on NES,
   SNES NTSC and PC Engine.

3. **The "never worse" guarantee is withdrawn.** It is worse than plain integer
   on SNES PAL and Genesis, measurably and substantially. Anyone choosing
   between the two is making a real trade, not taking a free upgrade.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The display table is now generated rather than transcribed, so this
class of decay is detectable. `make check` fails if the pinned core set moves.

**Harder.** Seven modes, and one of them now needs a caveat rather than a
one-line description. A launcher presenting `integer-vertical` as strictly
better than `integer` would be lying on two of seven rows.

**Foreclosed.** Nothing. Neither mode is removed.

**Open, and named rather than quietly left.** When the shape-correct width
overflows the panel, dropping a vertical factor is one of three options and was
never actually chosen - it fell out of the arithmetic. The others are clamping
the width, which abandons shape correctness at exactly the point the mode exists
to protect it, and cropping horizontally, which the port already supports since
rects may extend past the surface. **Cropping looks better than losing 60% of
the panel on Genesis**, but that is a judgement about how it looks, and this
project has been wrong before by settling those from a table.

## Revisit if

- Someone looks at Genesis and SNES PAL on the panel in `integer-vertical` and
  judges the dropped factor better or worse than a crop. That closes the open
  item above, and it needs eyes rather than arithmetic.
- A core is added or replaced whose reported aspect is wider than 1.5842 or
  narrower than 1.1111, which would extend the range the modes must cover.
- `tools/corefacts.sh --check` reports a change, which means this table is
  describing binaries the project no longer runs - the exact failure that made
  this ADR necessary.
